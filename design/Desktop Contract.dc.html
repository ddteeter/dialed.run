The phone owns the act. Desktop owns the table.

Promoted from a note on screen X to the system's position. Desktop is a reading and closet-admin surface. Every act of logging — a run, a verdict, a garment — is the phone flow, unchanged, in a centred panel at phone width. Exactly one job earns a wide layout it didn't have: clearing a backlog of verdicts, because a keyboard and a table beat six sheets.

DS0 · Does the doctrine hold Yes, across all eight v1 areas — with four places it bends. None of them is a second wide form.
BEND 1 · THE CAMERA ISN'T THERE

A1 upload and F add-garment open the camera on phone. At width the same panel shows a drop zone in the photo well — "Drop a photo, or shoot it on your phone later." Copy and one state change; the layout is untouched. Face-blur runs the same WASM path on the dropped file.

BEND 2 · ONBOARDING SKIPS O2

Shoot-the-closet is a phone act. Desktop onboarding runs O1 → O3 → O4 → O5 in the centred panel and O5 adds one line: "Photos come from your phone — we'll remind you." The ladder (O6) does not lose a rung; the closet just fills from the tap-list first.

BEND 3 · ONE WIDE JOB

The verdict backlog. A3 is a phone sheet for one run; six imported runs missing an outfit is a table. DS2 draws it. The row is A3's inputs laid flat — outfit, verdict, save — not a new form. Editing an outfit still opens A2 in the panel.

BEND 4 · THE HEADER BECOMES THE BAR

Phone screens carry an ink header block with the title, the bell and (on You) the theme segment. At width there is one top bar; per-screen headers drop to a plain TYPE.title above the content. Bell and theme segment move to the bar — see DS1.

Where it does not bend: the Call. The reveal (brackets open, layers arrive in dressing order) is designed for one column at phone width and stays there. The Call on desktop is the centred panel plus its summary card in the feed rail. A wide Call would be a dashboard, and a dashboard is the opposite of an answer.

WHAT REPLACES THE TAB BAR

The four tabs become four text links in the bar, same order, same active rule (pink underline, no crossfade — motion.js "Tab switch" applies unchanged). Not a sidebar: the left rail is the Desk's chrome and is what marks a screen as operator-only. The product never grows one.

THE BELL AND THE WORDMARK

The bell leaves the per-screen header and sits in the bar, right side, with its S2 states unchanged. It opens the S1 list as a centred panel, not a dropdown. The bracketed wordmark is TYPE.title and links to Feed; the phone has no wordmark on-screen, so this is the one place the logo appears in the product.

LOG A RUN IS ALWAYS IN THE BAR

The phone's floating action sits in the tab bar; at width it's the pink pill in the top bar, on every screen. It opens A1 in the centred panel. Search is the same field the Following empty-state uses — username search, not discovery.

THE BAR INVERTS WITH THE GROUND

Ink bar on paper, paper bar on ink — T2 rule 04 applied to the one header that remains. The theme segment lives here because it stopped living in the You header. Pink-as-text on the paper bar is --cold-text, so the bracket goes #C21A6B in dark's paper bar exactly as T1 says.

The panel rule, precisely. Width 390. Sheet radius (20). Ground behind it is --ground, the panel is the phone screen's own surfaces — no scrim, no shadow, no device bezel. It sits SPACE[12] below the bar, top-aligned, never vertically centred (a flow's first step and its fifth should start at the same y). Its own header keeps the phone's title and back control; only the bell and tab bar are gone. Escape = back.

The reflow rule, precisely. Same markup, one column, max-width: var(--measure-column), left-aligned inside the page measure — not centred, so it lines up with the wide screens' primary column. Screen padding SPACE[5] → SPACE[6]. Lists keep their row height; grids of runs or garments go from 2 tracks to auto-fill, minmax(180px, 1fr). The ink header block becomes a TYPE.title line. That is the whole rule; if a screen needs more, it isn't a reflow and belongs in the wide column of this table.

DS4 · Breakpoints & T1 at width Two thresholds, three layouts. Exported from tokens.js as BREAKPOINT and MEASURE. The board widths you asked about: 1180 is the system's page measure; 620 is the column measure; 980 and 1440 are not tokens.
Phone< 720

The screens as drawn. Tab bar, ink header blocks, sheets from the bottom edge. Fluid from 320 up; nothing pins a width.

Wide≥ 720 · BREAKPOINT.wide

Top bar replaces tab bar (search and theme collapse to icons). Every surface is one column: reflow screens at 620, acts in the 390 panel. Sheets that came from the bottom edge become the panel. No rails yet.

Desk≥ 1040 · BREAKPOINT.desk

The three wide layouts get their second column (1.55fr / 1fr, gap SPACE[6]). Content max 1180 inside SPACE[6] gutters. Bar shows the full search field and the theme segment. Everything else looks exactly like Wide.

Why 720 and 1040. 720 is the narrowest width at which a 620 column and SPACE[6] gutters fit with room for the bar's four links plus two icons and a pill — below it the bar would need to collapse into a menu, which is the tab bar with extra steps. 1040 is 620 + 24 + 372 + 24: the primary column at its measure, a rail no narrower than the phone panel, and the gutters. A rail narrower than a phone screen would be a widget strip.

T1 at width: nothing resolves differently. A ground is a ground. Two notes, not exceptions: the top bar is the ink-block header and follows T2 rule 04 (inverts with the ground), so the bracket in a paper bar is --cold-text not --action. And the panel sits directly on --ground with no scrim — a scrim would be a third grey, and the phone screen already has its own surface stack.
