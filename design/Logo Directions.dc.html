08 Within tolerance

Brackets. Engineering notation for "inside spec." Dialed means the answer fell between the bounds — and the brackets then become a system for every range the product shows.

WEAKNESSBrackets around a wordmark is a known move, and it can read developer-tool rather than sport.

11 Dead centre

A spirit level with the bubble exactly between the guides. Universally understood as "precisely right" — and the only mark here that reads correct-versus-incorrect at a glance.

STRENGTHThe clearest meaning of any direction: nobody needs it explained. Off-centre states give you an honest empty/error/loading vocabulary for free.
WEAKNESSA level is a trade tool — carpentry, not running. Wide horizontal shape fights square icon slots and needs the bubble alone below 24px.
Round 2 recommendation

Direction 08 — brackets — as the system, direction 07 — registration — as the mark.

08 is the one that escapes being a logo and becomes a way of writing: [38–46°], [8 OF 9], [DIALED] — precision expressed everywhere the product shows a range, which is everywhere. It's type-only, so there's nothing to redraw per sport. 07 supplies the standalone icon that 08 lacks and shares its logic: something landing exactly inside bounds. Against round 1, both beat the dial on meaning and beat the indicator dot on system reach.

If you want the loudest option rather than the smartest one, take 10 — flush left. It has the least idea in it and the most confidence, which for a sport brand is a legitimate trade.

02 The indicator dot

The tittle on the "i" becomes an indicator light — the dot that goes on when something is set correctly. One character carries the whole idea, and the dot-over-stem detaches into an icon.

STRENGTHCheapest possible intervention with a real payoff. Gives you an icon that isn't a monogram. Animates naturally — the dot lights when a call resolves.
WEAKNESSDetached icon is abstract on its own — needs the wordmark nearby for a while before it earns recognition.
MY PICKHighest ratio of meaning to intervention. Survives every TLD and every one-color context.

03 The dial

A literal control knob with the needle parked off-vertical — set, not centered. The most explicitly on-name mark of the set.

STRENGTHInstantly explains the name. Reads as an instrument, which supports the utility half of the brand. Rotation is a free loading state.
WEAKNESSCircle-plus-needle is crowded territory — reads adjacent to camera apps, thermostats, and audio tools. Loses the needle below 20px.
VERDICTThe obvious answer, with the risks that usually come with obvious. Worth a trademark search before falling for it.

Five bars, tallest in the middle — the temperature-band chart that appears throughout the product, promoted to a mark. The peak is the sweet spot: dialed.

06 The tick

A dial unrolled into a flat scale, with one tick taller and pink — the setting you landed on. Same idea as the dial, none of the crowded circle territory.

STRENGTHDegrades gracefully all the way to a single pink tick, which is about as ownable as a 16px mark gets. Reads as an instrument scale, not a chart.
WEAKNESSQuieter than the brand's voice wants. Thin strokes are fragile on merch, embroidery, and against photography.
VERDICTThe most refined option, and the least loud. Strong if the brand matures toward instrument, weak if it stays race-day.

Round 1 recommendation  ·  superseded by round 2

Direction 02 — the indicator dot — as the wordmark, with direction 06's tick as the standalone icon.

02 costs one modified character and gives the name a visual idea that no competitor can copy without copying the name. 06 covers what 02 can't: a mark that works with no word attached, down to 16px, in one colour. They share a logic — a single element marking the right setting — so they read as one system rather than two logos. Skip 03 unless a trademark search comes back clean; skip 05 as anything but merch.

NEXT STEP

These are direction studies in a system font, not final artwork. A type designer should draw the "i" and the tick as real vectors with proper optical spacing.

BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Trademark search on the dial (03) and the bars (04) — both sit in crowded classes. The dot and tick are far safer ground.

TEST

Put the icons on a phone home screen next to Strava and a weather app. The one you can still find in half a second wins.
