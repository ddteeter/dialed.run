WHY A TEASER AND NOT A HIDDEN TAB

A missing tab teaches nothing. This one makes the value proposition legible from day one and gives every verdict a visible destination — the countdown is the only retention mechanic v1 has.

HI-VIZ DISCIPLINE

Two yellow moments only: the countdown and the "log this next" label. Yellow means the app is telling you something, and on this screen it's telling you exactly two things.

THE THRESHOLD IS A PLACEHOLDER

It should come out of the hand-validation exercise, not the design — so it's a tweak. Drag it (and the logged count) to see how the countdown reads at 10, 15, or 25. An early call that's wrong costs more trust than a longer wait.

TRANSITION OUT

When the threshold is met, this screen is replaced by B1 — and the coverage block moves to the profile (G). Don't keep both.

THE NOTIFICATION

No distance, no pace, no time of day — the notification carries nothing from Strava, because nothing from Strava is stored. It opens +Add, where the run gets built from a file or by hand.

CONNECTION BROKEN
Strava[RECONNECT NEEDED]

Strava revoked our access — usually a password change. Reminders stopped 4 days ago.

Also surfaces as one dismissible banner on the feed. Silent failure is the real risk here — a broken connection looks identical to "no runs" otherwise.

SAY WHAT YOU DON'T TAKE

"What we store from Strava: nothing" is the most valuable sentence on the screen. Runners are rightly suspicious of apps asking for Strava access, and the honest answer is a competitive advantage.

WHY IT'S NOT AN IMPORT

Terms of use make Strava-as-a-data-source unavailable, so GPX upload stays the only intake path. Connecting only buys the trigger — the design has to make that limitation feel like restraint rather than a gap.

OPEN QUESTION

A notification that leads to a manual file upload is a rough handoff. Worth testing whether runners tolerate it, or whether the nudge should simply come from a schedule they set.

IF TREADMILL  ·  [INDOOR]

Conditions are replaced by an optional room-temp field, and the run is tagged [INDOOR]. It counts in your mileage and your kit history, but it doesn't train the outdoor model — and the screen says so.

A segmented control at the top of +Add, not a buried fallback. Most runners have never exported a GPX and shouldn't be blocked on learning how.

MANUAL ENTRY IS NOT A DOWNGRADE

Time plus city gets conditions accurate enough to be useful. Treating hand entry as first-class removes the biggest onboarding cliff in the product — and every hand-entered run still teaches the model.

WHY NAMING EARNED A STEP

A generic "long sleeve" can only ever inherit a category average. A named piece accumulates its own range — so the identity layer is what makes the closet worth building. Two or three pieces is enough to prove the idea without turning onboarding into data entry.

WHAT THIS COSTS

No seeded history means every new user starts at zero coverage, so the countdown in K is the only thing standing between install and value. It has to be honest and it has to feel reachable.
