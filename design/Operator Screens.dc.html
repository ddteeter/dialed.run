The Desk.

Decision first: dialed.run has an admin section. Four surfaces reached by four memorised URLs is four places for one to be forgotten, and the daily digest needs somewhere to link. The Desk is one route, /desk, behind the existing admin check, with its own shell. It is never linked from the runner app — an operator knows the address, the way they always did.

THREE ITEMS, NOT FOUR

Banning isn't a destination; it's something you do to a runner. It lives on the runner's page under Runners. The digest isn't a destination either — it's Today, and the email is Today sent to you.

HI-VIZ IS THE DESK'S ACCENT

Pink stays the runner app's action colour. On the Desk the active rail item, counts that need a person, and anything the screener flagged use #F5FF3D. Approve and Remove are ink and hairline — neither is the "good" button.

WHAT IT'S NOT

No dashboard, no filters, no date pickers, no charts. One person opens this. If it ever needs more, that's the hiring problem W3c already named.

SEVERAL PHOTOS

One row per reported thing, never per photo. A reported photo shows large with a hi-viz frame and its siblings small for context; a reported entry shows all photos at one size. The Remove button names its scope — "this photo" or "this entry" — so the operator never has to infer it.

AFTER THE DECISION

The row collapses in place to one struck-through line: what it was, the decision, the time, who. It stays until the day rolls over, with Undo live the whole time. Nothing vanishes — a decision you can't see is a decision you can't check.

WHO REPORTED

Visible, behind a fold, and opening the fold is logged. Coordinated reporting is itself a moderation case and the reviewer needs to be able to see it. The promise to reporters is that the author never learns — W1 and W3c now say that precisely.

HOW A GROUP READS

Brand in mono, then the most-used name in bold as the probable canonical, then every variant beneath at regular weight with its own count. The bold name is a fact about usage, not a merge proposal — the screen deliberately can't act.

SCANNING MANY

Cards in a fill grid, ordered by pieces affected, so the worst dirt is top-left. One header line carries the whole state: names, probable products, pieces. That number moving is the only thing an operator is here to watch.

ONE CONFIRMATION, NOT TWO

The required reason is the confirmation — nobody writes two sentences to someone by accident. No modal, no "are you sure", no typing the handle back.

THE FIELD LABEL DOES THE WORK

"They will read this exactly as written" is the whole moderation policy in one label. It makes the operator write to a person, and it means the notice never needs a second, softer version of the reason.

AFTER

The runner's page stays open with a struck-through header and BANNED · 14:07 · @mara — same pattern as a decided review row. No landing page, no toast.

CASE [B-0031]
"CLOSED", NOT "BANNED"

Not softer — more accurate. "Banned" describes the operator's act; "closed" describes the state the runner is now in, which is what the heading needs to say. The body still uses "a person closed it" so no one can read it as a system event.

NO BUTTON

There is nothing to do inside the app, so the screen offers nothing to tap. The appeal route is an address and a stated response time — a promise a person keeps, not a form that lands in a queue nobody drew.

NO PINK, NO HI-VIZ

Ink on paper only. Pink is action and there is none; hi-viz is failure and this isn't one. Nothing animates — this is the doctrine's deliberately static case at its most literal.

D5 · The digest An email at 07:00 local, every day including the days there is nothing — so a missing email is a broken pipeline, never a quiet one. Its body is the Desk's Today, and its one link goes there.

Open the Desk

Sent every morning, even when every number is zero. If it stops arriving, something is broken.

WHAT THEY READ

Three numbers, each with one sentence that says what the number costs. The subject line carries the two that matter so the email is often read without being opened. No list of items — the Desk has those.

WHY ALWAYS SEND

The rule is that a failure must reach a human. A digest that only sends when something happened can't tell the human that the digest itself has died. A zero day reads "Nothing waiting. Nothing failed." — and that sentence arriving is the check.

FOR THE LANES

Plain HTML email, one template, system font fallback acceptable. /desk renders the same three numbers from the same query, so Today and the digest cannot disagree.
