The outfit is the subject; the face is incidental. Detection runs on-device, so the unblurred frame never leaves the phone — but it reports what it found rather than promising it found everything, and the runner can blur anything by hand.

NOTHING TO BLUR

FEASIBILITY · FOR THE LANES

On-device face detection ships with both platforms and needs no new dependency or server round trip: detect, draw the blur into a new image, upload that. Where it gets soft is recall — profiles, sunglasses under a cap, distant mirror shots, several people in frame. That's why the manual tap exists and why no copy says "faces are blurred".

Mobile web has no equivalent worth shipping. If a browser upload path opens, it posts unblurred or not at all — decide that before the web feed does.

THE STANCE

A person reviews every report, within a day, and the reporter is never named. No automated takedowns, no strike counters, no public flags on entries. If the volume ever outgrows a person, that's a hiring problem, not a product one.

WHY IT'S ITS OWN OBJECT

Every other garment accumulates a temperature range. A shoe accumulates wear, and its useful life ends on a number the runner chooses. Same closet, same rows, different detail screen — and the recommendation engine keeps treating shoes as a category with no thermal opinion.

NO GUESSED LIFESPAN

Every shoe tracker invents a mileage limit and then argues with the runner about it. Ours ships with a default the runner sets once and can change forever, and the bar is a fact rather than a verdict.

HONEST ARITHMETIC

Miles come from logged runs only. Saying so in one line is cheaper than reconciling with Strava's total and losing either way.

STILL OPEN

Multiple pairs of the same model, retired-shoe history, and whether a treadmill run counts toward wear at all. None of it blocks the object existing.
