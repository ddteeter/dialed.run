FEASIBILITY · FOR THE LANES

On-device face detection ships with both platforms and needs no new dependency or server round trip: detect, draw the blur into a new image, upload that. Where it gets soft is recall — profiles, sunglasses under a cap, distant mirror shots, several people in frame. That's why the manual tap exists and why no copy says "faces are blurred".

Decided in round 8: the browser keeps the promise and changes the delivery — a WASM detector, on by default, the blurred canvas uploaded, the original never leaving the device. The two states the web adds are drawn in AD.

FOR THE LANES
01wardrobe_items.type is a cache of products.category_hint. It is written on match and on enrichment, never by a user.
02No parser. Free text is never mined for type — not on write, not on read.
03Type chips come from a distinct query over the category's populated types. A category where every piece is generic shows no type chips at all — correct, not broken.
04Detail subtitle: type ?? categoryLabel + " · GENERIC". One expression, one slot.
05Type never affects a recommendation. Category and the learned range do that. Type is for finding things.
WHY NOT ASK

A second one-tap row is cheap to build and expensive every single time. F's whole argument is that identity is the only thing worth a tap, because identity is what pays the runner back. Charging a tap for a filter facet inverts that.

WHY NOT PARSE

A parser is a feature that works until it doesn't, fails invisibly, and can't be corrected by the person looking at the wrong answer. Nullable and honest beats populated and wrong.

AH3 · THE RULE · FOR THE LANES
PASSES OR FAILS A PAIR
01Two classes. Neutral: black, white, grey, navy, brown, beige. Colour: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink. Thirteen names, locked; no "multi", no "other".
02Name rule. A kit fails if it holds two different Colour names. Neutrals pair with anything. Same name twice passes. Null passes — no colour means no test.
03Hex refinement, when both pieces carry one. Convert to OKLCH. C < 0.06 is Neutral whatever it was named. Two Colours with the same name pass only if ΔE_ok ≤ 0.06 — a set. Farther is the near-miss, and it fails. Starting thresholds; tune on real closets.
04Safety is outside the rule. A garment with visibility = hi-viz is invisible to the test — not Neutral, not Colour, not counted. It's safety because the runner said so, never because a hex is bright. Reflective trim is not exempt; the base colour is what shows.
05Scope. Top, bottom, outer — the three layers that show. Hats, gloves, buffs, socks, shoes are exempt.
06Where it runs. After the thermal ranking, among candidates within the same band. Prefer a passing kit; if none passes, take the thermal best and say nothing. Colour never demotes a warmer-right answer and never appears in a reason on the Call face.
07The one place the word may appear in Call copy: the "why not" sheet, when the tiebreak decided. "Same warmth. Went with the black under the red top." Prose, a name, no swatch.
08Before the Call: garment detail carries the name on the identity line — LONG SLEEVE · NAVY · OBSIDIAN — the way AG carries composition. Not the closet grid, not a filter, no swatch. Enrichment may propose the name as an editable claim (F2c); it never proposes a hex.
WHY NEVER SHOW IT

AB fixed hue as verdict and the Call is the screen where verdict hue does its hardest work — brackets, layers in dressing order. A navy swatch beside a teal bracket is a third accent that means nothing. Verdict hue on the Call is untouched because nothing competes with it.

WHY STRICT IS FREE

"One Colour per kit" is stricter than any runner's taste, and it costs nothing: the rule only breaks ties, so its worst case is picking the black tights over the navy ones. A permissive rule that reasons about which colours "go" is the fashion app the Brief forbids.

WHY TWO FIDELITIES, ONE RULE

The hex never overrides the name rule; it only tightens it — reclassifying a dull "blue" as Neutral, or failing the two pinks the name rule can't see. A runner who gave one field gets a coarser Call, not a different one.

THE WORDS

Berlin-Kay's eleven, plus navy and beige because that's what's on the hang tag. Hi-viz is cut from the list: it isn't a colour, it's a reason, and the VISIBILITY attribute already holds it. "Shade" for level 2; never "tone", "palette", "match".
