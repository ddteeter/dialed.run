RULE · THE HOUR

A Call answers for a specific hour. It is never a daily high or a daily range. The hour is usual by default (median start of the last 20 logged runs, quarter-hour), now when there are fewer than five runs, and always visible in the header pill. Forecast, cohort lookup and coverage band are all resolved against that hour. A lane that renders a Call without a resolved hour has a bug.

RULE · ATTRIBUTION

Every card that shows a forecast or a conditions reading carries WEATHER · VISUAL CROSSING as its last line, linked to visualcrossing.com, in 9px mono at the card's quiet colour. Same screen, same card, never only in Settings — that is Visual Crossing's requirement, not ours. The Settings footer line stays as a courtesy, not as the attribution.

ONE SHELL, TWO STATES

The header row is identical above and below the threshold — place picker, day picker, Trip entry. Only the body changes. That's what makes K's replacement a state and not a redesign.

B1 FOLDS IN

The old B1 form becomes the header pill. It arrives pre-filled with the runner's usual time — the median start of their last 20 logged runs, to the quarter hour — so the common case is still zero taps. Tapping the pill opens a row: NOW, USUAL, or a picker. Effort and distance sit behind a tap on the forecast card. A new runner with no history gets NOW. One sentence under the forecast card says whose time it is and that it's tappable; it stops showing once the runner has changed the time once.

THE GLYPH

call in icons.js — brackets around a pin with a reading. Sixth bracketed glyph by exception. Swapping it in for verdictPending is the epic's first visible commit.

"BORROWED", NOT "BEST GUESS"

O5's disclosure says the app doesn't know you yet. This one says it knows you fine — it just doesn't know you at 84°. Different sentence, same slot, same hi-viz-on-ink treatment. The coverage bar marks the target band in hi-viz so the gap is visible, not just stated.

COHORT IS THE DESTINATION'S

Minneapolis runners at 84° are a thin, strange sample. Denver runners at 84° are the population that matters. Every borrowed line names its source.

TIME IS NOT OPTIONAL

6 AM and 6 PM on the same day can be 20° apart. A Call without an hour is a daily high — the dressing chart we exist to beat. So the hour is always set; it's just set for you by default. USUAL is learned, never asked in onboarding.

ALTITUDE AND UV

Two conditions a home runner never sees. They appear in the reading line when they matter and drive one recommendation each — never a lecture.

SHOP HINTS · THE RULE

A gap names the category and what the destination cohort wears — brands only as "fits your other pieces," drawn from products already in the runner's closet. No prices, no links, no affiliate anything in this epic. The hint's job is to make the gap actionable, not to sell into it. Milestone 4 earns commerce; this doesn't.

WHEN THE FORECAST ISN'T THERE
Place not foundFIELD ERROR
Form Contract: hi-viz band under the place field, "Try a city or a postcode." Nothing renders below until it resolves.
Fetch failedHAIRLINE DAY
The day goes hairline. The Call still renders from the runner's bands and the cohort's seasonal norm, with the reading line replaced by NO FORECAST · USING DENVER'S JULY NORMAL. "I can't help" is never the output.
Past 15 daysNOT AN ERROR
Hatch state. Range shown, hour estimated, verbs hedged. Re-fetched daily; a day flips to solid on its own once it enters the 15-day window, and the packing list may change — the runner is told if it does.
DAY STRIP USES COVERAGE INK

Solid, hatch, hairline — the coverage bar's three states, now meaning forecast confidence. Solid: hourly forecast, within 15 days; the Call is for the hour. Hatch: past 15 days, Visual Crossing returns a statistical day built from decades of history — a range, not a number, and no hourly row, so the hour is estimated from the climate curve. The strip shows the range and the Call says "probably". Hairline: no data — fetch failed or place unresolved. There is no maximum range out; a trip next August is a hatch trip.

"COPY LIST", NOT "SHARE"

The list goes to a notes app or a partner. Plain text, one line per piece with the days. Under AF's rule the button says Copied for [2000ms] and reverts — no toast.

FOR THE LANES

trips(id, runner, place, start, end) is the whole table. Forecast is cached per place-day, never stored on the trip. Deleting a trip deletes nothing else.

BACKGROUND

A vision model turns the photo into structured attributes: category, sleeve length, fabric class, weight, wind/water resistance, color. Those attributes feed a thermal-property table that produces an estimated temp range with no run history at all. Multi-garment photos are segmented before classification. The model's output is always presented as a claim the user can correct — corrections are training data.

This is the only place AI touches the recommendation, and it's doing data entry — not guessing how cold you'll feel.

BACKGROUND

The GPX archive is parsed in bulk, then every run's start coordinate and timestamp hit a historical weather API to backfill conditions — this is why 148 runs can arrive already carrying temperature, humidity, wind, and precipitation. A memorability heuristic ranks runs by extremity and recency (coldest, hottest, wettest, most recent) because those are the ones a human can actually recall an outfit for. Each tagged run becomes a full training example: conditions + kit + implied "worked."

The highest-leverage screen in onboarding. Four taps buys a temperature range no new user could otherwise cover for months.

BACKGROUND

A blended prior: cohort distribution (what similar runners wore in these conditions), the user's seeded runs, their self-reported offset, and a published dressing heuristic as the floor. Weights shift automatically as personal data accumulates — the source bars are literally the model's weighting, exposed. The engine also computes a confidence score from history count and conditions coverage, which drives the header language and the "probably" hedge.

Showing the sources is the trust mechanism. A recommendation that admits what it's leaning on is much harder to resent when it's wrong.
