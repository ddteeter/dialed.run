Motion doctrine  /  v1.0  /  Sep 2026 Source: motion.js

Things
arrive. They
don't settle.

Motion in dialed.run is confirmation, not decoration. Every move answers one question — did that register? — and then gets out of the way. Nothing bounces, because wobble is the opposite of dialed.

LAW 01 Things arrive, they don't settle.

No bounce, no spring, no overshoot. Curves decelerate to a dead stop. Nothing in the product is uncertain about where it lands.

LAW 02 The bracket does the work.

Reveals, commits and waits are expressed by the brackets closing, opening or breathing — not by generic fades and slides.

LAW 03 Nothing travels far.

24px for an element, one bracket width for a frame. Containers may travel their own height. Nothing crosses the screen.

LAW 04

Every navigation has a direction, and the direction is the transition.

Forward comes from the trailing edge. Back leaves the way it came. A flow rises from the bar and drops back to it. A swap into a pane has no direction, so it gets no travel. Five types cover every shipped edge, including the ones the Flow Map never drew; a lane meeting a new edge types it by analogy and adds the row in the same PR.

WHY THE PHONE SLIDES AND THE DESKTOP DOESN'T

On a phone the new screen replaces the old one, so travel says where the old one went. At 620+ the Desktop Contract keeps the shell and swaps a column or opens a panel — the old content didn't go anywhere, so sliding it would claim a geometry the layout doesn't have.

THE CALL IS TWO MOVES, NOT ONE

Arriving at K is a plain push or panel. The brackets open only once it has landed. Chaining them would make the reveal a navigation effect; it's the payoff, and it waits for the room to be quiet.

REDUCED MOTION

Every type becomes swap at instant. Cut stays cut. Never zero — the runner still needs to see the screen change.
