# Legitimate-interests assessment: published anonymous totals

> **DRAFT: not reviewed by a lawyer.** The balancing note behind the privacy
> policy's "Anonymous totals" section, written 2026-10-11 from D-108, D-119
> and D-120 for the owner to keep on file. It is a record, not public text.

## What the processing is

[dialed.run] adds up a few fields of every counted run, shared and private,
and publishes the totals as guides and reports under CC BY 4.0 (D-101,
D-105, D-108, D-119, D-120). The fields are the feels-like temperature
band, sky, month, garment type and catalogue model, verdict, and the broad
climate zone (Köppen-Geiger) the run was in, looked up from its rounded
location when the totals are made. Runners may also be grouped by the
conditions they usually run in, worked out from their own counted runs.
Nothing else is read: no notes, photos, routes, start times, start points,
handles or other free text, and no place more precise than the zone. There
is no self-declared region.

## 1. Purpose

Guides that tell runners what to wear in given conditions, built from what
runners actually wore and how it went. Counting private runs is the point,
not a side effect: a guide built only from shared runs is built from the
runs people were proud of, and it overstates "dialed" exactly where a runner
needs the truth (design round 32). The interest is ours (a useful public
product that brings runners to [dialed.run]) and runners' (better guides,
including for the runners counted). It is lawful and specific. The totals
are not sold.

## 2. Necessity

- **Shared runs alone do not serve the purpose**, for the bias reason above.
- **The fields are the minimum the guides use.** Each one is a column the
  guides print; there is no field read "in case".
- **Aggregation is the least intrusive form.** No figure is about one
  person, and no row-level data leaves the app.
- **Consent would defeat it.** An off-by-default setting counts only runners
  who opt in, and that brings back the same self-selection bias.

## 3. Balancing

What a runner might reasonably expect, and the risks to them, against the
safeguards:

| Risk or expectation                                   | Safeguard                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "Private" read as "never used"                        | Disclosed where the choice is made: A3's PRIVATE line, Settings › Privacy and the sign-up legal line (D-108 E); privacy policy |
| A figure small enough to point at one person          | 5 or more distinct runners per figure, 20 for a brand, for every row and slice; a cell below the line is left out (D-108 B)    |
| Re-identification from the fields                     | Only the counted fields; no names, handles, notes, photos, times or places, and no row-level output (D-108 A)                  |
| Location narrowing a figure                           | Only a Köppen-Geiger zone, coarser than a state; nothing new stored; a zone or group slice meets the same threshold (D-120)    |
| Accounts that are not real, or that are leaving       | Confirmed accounts only; closed and leaving accounts, removed or quarantined kits and runner-set conditions left out (D-108 C) |
| Wanting out                                           | One switch, on by default, off at any time; off leaves future guides and reports, and deleting the account is an opt-out (D)   |
| Reuse under CC BY 4.0 by others                       | Only the published totals are licensed, and they hold nothing about any one runner                                             |
| A report already published cannot drop someone's runs | It names no one and cannot be unpicked; the setting stops future ones                                                          |

Replacing a self-declared state with a climate zone strengthens this
balance: a zone usually spans several states, so a slice by zone holds more
runners than a slice by state would, and the runner discloses nothing new.
A state guide on the marketing site maps the state to its zones without
any runner data.

The data is not sensitive (no health data: a verdict is comfort, not a
symptom), runners are adults (D-114), and the output is anonymous. The
residual impact on any one runner is minimal.

## 4. Conclusion

Legitimate interests (GDPR / UK GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) is the basis. The
interest is real, counting private runs is necessary to it, and with the
safeguards above runners' interests do not override it. The setting is how a
runner objects (Art. 21), and it takes effect without the runner having to
give a reason. Revisit if the counted fields, the thresholds or the opt-out
default change, or if totals start including anything about closets beyond
pieces worn on counted runs.
